QSM Direct stock4 — stable reference detail during network-rate changes
2026-10-23

Problem and reproduction
------------------------
The earlier isolated-loss fix prevents noisy RR samples from changing the
encoder target, but genuine target changes still reset NVENC rate control
and force an IDR through FFmpeg. VA-API/QSV rate changes reopen their codec.
With a one-frame VBV, repeated refreshes also change static reference detail.
Hardware frame-size overshoot and a four-frame transport FIFO can then cause
additional local drops/recovery requests. This is more than a network-loss
threshold problem.

The exact production LibavVideoEncoder class encoded a generated, unchanging
2560x1440 text/colour fixture at 60 fps. Target phases were 8000, 2000, 8000,
1500 and 8000 kbit/s, with no network transport:
  Old H.264 NVENC: 600 decoded pictures, five IDRs, maximum whole-picture
    frame-to-frame mean luma change 11.5245/255. Several subsequent P-frames
    changed horizontal regions too. Actual low-target output exceeded its
    target: about 3.217 Mbit/s at 2000 kbit/s, 2.390 at 1500 kbit/s.
  New H.264 NVENC: 392 decoded pictures, one IDR, maximum change 0.0169393/255.
  New HEVC NVENC: 384 decoded pictures, one IDR, maximum change 0.00957086/255.

The new probe changes the admission budget, retaining the configured encoder
settings. Its synthetic clock covers the same ten-second source interval;
it is a codec/decoded-pixel check, not a real-time browser benchmark.

Implementation
--------------
VideoBudget charges each complete encoded access unit, including keyframes,
with a transport reserve before admitting another raw picture. Under load,
newer raw scanouts replace older ones before encoding. No predictive encoded
picture is discarded merely to meet the target. Cheap static pictures can
still run at the negotiated cadence. Motion cadence falls when the budget
cannot carry the current pictures, preserving static reference detail.

Network changes no longer call set_bitrate/reopen the codec. Configured
maximum bitrate and resolution are still chosen when starting the producer.
Real recovery requests, a new viewer or a resize may still require an IDR.
The common worker path covers VM/LXC, H.264/HEVC and the supported encoders.
Intel/AMD hardware was not requalified in this change.

Timed private video records carry a monotonic 90-kHz timestamp. The bridge
retains a fixed-cadence fallback for old records and rebases explicit times
per viewer. Skipped raw ticks therefore do not compress the RTP clock or
accumulate presentation delay. The audio/input/cursor protocols are unchanged.

RTP/RTX pacing no longer adds the old hardware overshoot allowance at low
targets; producer admission now pays that debt. The normal 25% pacing margin
remains. Fresh REMB bounds healthy upward probes without reducing a healthy
target on an app-limited estimate. This prevents automatic recovery from
continually overrunning a path which has settled at a lower capacity.
