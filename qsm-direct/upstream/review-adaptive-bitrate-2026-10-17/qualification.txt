QSM Direct adaptive bitrate qualification — 2026-10-17

Scope
-----
One adaptive controller per encoded producer (VM, or LXC display slot), shared
by all viewers. RTCP input is authenticated by the existing DTLS/SRTP peer;
only reports for the actual video SSRC affect its controller. REMB estimates,
RR cumulative-loss deltas and raw matching SR/RR RTT samples drive bounded
AIMD. Encoder target 500..100000 kbit/s; existing saved bitrate is the ceiling.
No extra listener, port, guest agent permission or Proxmox API schema change.

Decreases are limited to at most once per second; loss >=12% halves the target,
other congestion reduces it by 25%, with a fresh falling REMB also supplying
a ceiling. RR loss >=3% or RTT > baseline + max(25ms, 20% baseline) signals
congestion. Growth waits three healthy seconds, then adds 8%/>=100 kbit/s per
second without REMB, or probes by 5%/>=100 kbit/s every two seconds with REMB.
Fresh feedback is mandatory. A constant/increasing app-limited REMB must not
repeatedly lower the encoder during recovery; its first/significantly falling
value is honoured for 2.5 seconds. Silent peers expire after six seconds;
closed peers are removed immediately. No controller timer/task survives close.

Live reconfiguration
--------------------
Private QSMI opcode 7, network-order uint32 kbit/s, updates an atomic mailbox.
The video thread alone changes AVCodecContext bitrate/maxrate/VBV before the
next submitted frame. Encoder object and frame timestamp sequence survive.
Supported: libx264, h264_nvenc and hevc_nvenc. HEVC/NVENC now uses that same
in-process path. QSV/VA-API remain fixed and Settings explicitly identifies it;
we do not restart an external FFmpeg process on every congestion report.
All viewers' video/RTX token buckets follow the shared target; audio, RTCP and
input bypass those buckets. Settings receives the active target/ceiling via
the existing unordered server cursor-state channel, with monotonic serials
that reject stale samples and a 10-second refresh. The reliable control channel
is used only until that latest-state channel opens.

NVENC limitation
----------------
The reference GPU can exceed low targets significantly: the synthetic moving
1280x800/60 probe measured 2.58 Mbps at target 2 and 1.31 Mbps at target 0.5.
Pacing permits extra low-rate credit max(0, 800 - target_kbps/10) kbit/s before
the existing 25% packet/recovery allowance. This disappears at >=8 Mbps and
does not increase the encoder target. It prevents a known driver overshoot
from continually overrunning the local four-frame queue. It is an empirical
allowance, not a guarantee for every GPU or a network bandwidth measurement.
Targets are not total wire-traffic caps. Audio/recovery/headers are additional.
This release does not adapt resolution/FPS. Links below the encoder's practical
minimum require a smaller console, lower FPS or software encoding.
Abrupt bandwidth changes can cause short recovery interruptions.

Encoder probe
-------------
encoder-probe.cpp embeds the exact production LibavVideoEncoder class.
Run on the PVE node after compiling against Debian trixie FFmpeg 7 libraries:
  g++ -std=c++20 -O2 encoder-probe.cpp -o encoder-probe \
      $(pkg-config --cflags --libs libavcodec libavutil libswscale)
  ./encoder-probe h264_nvenc 8000
  ./encoder-probe libx264 8000
  ./encoder-probe hevc_nvenc 8000
Single object, 1200 frames: 300 each at 8, 2, 12, 0.5 Mbps, forced IDRs at
frames 7/17/49. All 1200 decode in each run, no decoder reopen. Files include
segment measurements and forced-IDR checks. NVENC CBR/p4 experiments did not
remove its low-rate overshoot; production keeps the already qualified p1/VBR.
